My pick: make the website our front door, sold only on a monthly plan with tools built in, then sell everything else (SEO, photo and content, custom software) to clients once they're in. Below are four directions to test that against on Friday, and a way to choose between them.

## Where we are today

We sell three things today, and only websites has outside proof. Both our recurring lines are small or unproven.

| Line | Public price today | Paying outside clients | Proof we can show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Websites | From $750, mostly one-off | Yes | Jones Pressure Washing, 360 Degree Care, Iffers Pictures |
| SEO | From $200/mo | 3, on cheap plans | No strong results yet |
| Custom tools and apps | Not offered publicly | None | Our own PVS dashboard and Domani (about 88 users) |

The gap that matters: our best skill earns nothing yet, and our recurring revenue sits on our weakest service.

## How to decide

Whatever we go all in on has to pass all four tests. Sami's point covers the first; the other three keep it a business.

1. **We want to do it.** We'd happily spend most of our week on it for the next 3 years.
2. **We're clearly better at it.** We can show work a local competitor or a template builder can't match.
3. **Someone pays every month.** It brings in recurring revenue, not just one invoice.
4. **A stranger gets it in one line.** If an ad or a homepage can't explain it in one sentence, we can't market it.

Before Friday, we each score every direction below against these four tests on our own, then compare.

## Four directions

A and D can be combined. B and C are real alternatives that would change what we market.

| Direction | One line a stranger would hear | Who buys | Front door | Proof today | Biggest risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Websites that run your business** | "A website that books, quotes and keeps clients updated, on a monthly plan." | Local service businesses (home services, care, trades) | Website on a monthly plan | Strong: websites + our own tools | Monthly plans need more support time from 2 people |
| **B. Full-service growth partner** | "Website, photos, content and marketing, all handled." | Local businesses, B2C-facing | Website or a photo/content shoot | Weak: SEO is our least proven service | Hard to deliver results across many services; easy to look generic |
| **C. Custom software studio** | "We build the dashboards and apps your business runs on." | Companies of roughly 20–200 people with messy operations | Paid pilot or ops audit | In-house only: PVS dashboard, Domani | No paying clients; long sales cycles; hard to advertise |

- **Photography and marketing (Sami's point):** these fit as add-ons in A and D, or as the core of B. Open question: do we shoot and run marketing ourselves, or partner with someone who does?
- **B2B vs B2C:** A, B and D sell to businesses whose customers are consumers. C is pure B2B. Very few agencies our size do both well.

## Getting in the home

Sami's AT&T point works only if the website is hard to leave. A plain website is like a phone: easy to swap. A website the business runs on every day is like home security. Each step up the ladder makes us harder to replace and earns more per client.

1. **Website on a monthly plan.** This is the front door. Hosting, updates and support are included, so we're on the account every month.
2. **Tools built into the site.** Booking, quotes, client portal, owner dashboard. The business runs its day through us.
3. **Growth services.** SEO, photography, content and marketing, sold to clients who already trust us and whose data we can see.
4. **Custom software.** Bigger projects for clients who have outgrown off-the-shelf tools. This is where the large invoices come from.

We market only step 1 (with a taste of step 2) to strangers. Steps 3 and 4 are sold to existing clients through check-ins and reporting, not ads.

## A first-draft front-door offer

If we pick A or D, this is a starting point to argue with. The prices are placeholders I picked to start the discussion, not researched numbers. Check them against our own hours per project before quoting anyone.

| Plan | What's included | Setup | Monthly | Term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Launch | Custom site, hosting, updates, support, lead alerts | $1,500 | $149 | 12 months |
| Grow | Launch + one built-in tool (booking or quote calculator) + monthly report | $2,500 | $299 | 12 months |
| Operate | Grow + client portal or owner dashboard, quarterly strategy call | Custom quote | From $499 | 12 months |

- **Name ideas:** "Websites that work for you," "Your business, online and running," "The website your business runs on."
- **Why a setup fee + monthly:** the setup fee covers the build; the monthly keeps us in the home and replaces one-off $750 sites.
- **Existing clients:** offer to move them onto a plan when their sites next need work.

## What we'd stop doing

Going all in on one direction means saying no to some things. Under A or D, I'd propose:

- **One-off sites from $750.** They get us in the door but don't keep us there. Every new site comes with a monthly plan.
- **Selling SEO on its own.** It becomes an add-on for plan clients until our 3 current clients have results we can show; then we raise prices.
- **Listing every service on the homepage.** One clear offer up front; everything else lives on its own page or comes up in client check-ins.
- **Taking projects outside our chosen buyer.** A one-off job that doesn't fit is fine for cash, but it never shapes the marketing.
